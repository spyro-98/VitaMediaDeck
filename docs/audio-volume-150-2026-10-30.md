# Common volume range — 30 October 2026

All players now use 0–150%. AudioOut handles normal attenuation up to 100%
(unity / 0 dB at the app port); the console's physical volume setting still
applies. Above 100%, PCM gain rises linearly to 1.5× at 150%, with signed-16-bit
saturation of peaks. Existing callers requesting 300% are clamped to 150%.
This is a smaller real gain limit, not a relabelled 3× amplifier or automatic
loudness normalization. Already loud material can gain mostly peak distortion.

MP3/radio, FLAC, AvPlayer audio and HW/SW video share the gain contract. The
independently buildable decoder packages retain identical private gain headers,
checked by the regression runner. Decoder consumers apply current gain per
output grain, preserving the raw queued PCM; lowering boost cannot retain old
clipping or apply gain twice. Volume publication to those consumers is atomic.
Triangle mute retains the selected level. The UI marks the 100% boundary and
uses the warning colour for boost in all themes.

ASan/UBSan and TSan tests cover 9.8 million signed-sample cases, the app's actual
background gain functions, unity/port bounds, mute restoration and a quiet-sine
RMS ratio of 1.49997× at 150%. Extracted production HW/SW output functions verify
mono/stereo queued playback and immediate per-grain gain changes without input
mutation. The real music UI loop covers 0/150 limits in all eight themes and
IT/EN. Host tests measure digital samples, not Vita speaker/headphone loudness;
physical listening remains required.
